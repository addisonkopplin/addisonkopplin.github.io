What is in essentials, and price change since …

Essentials price index components, weights and price change.
Component (CPI-U)WeightPrice change

Essentials is a price index built from four CPI-U groups: food, shelter, energy and medical care. Each is weighted by its share of consumer spending in December 2025 (BLS relative importance), rescaled so the four add up to 100%. Together they are 64% of the full CPI basket. The weights are held fixed over the whole period.

Home prices for shelter swaps the CPI shelter index, which tracks rents and what owners would pay to rent their own home, for the FHFA House Price Index of what homes sell for. It shows the S&P 500 against the cost of buying a house instead of renting one.

S&P 500 values are monthly averages of daily closes. "With dividends reinvested" adds one-twelfth of the trailing 12-month dividend each month, as in Robert Shiller's total return series. Run with his inflation figures, this calculation reproduces his series to four digits. Dividends for the last three months are not yet published and are held at the latest value. No fees or taxes are taken out.

Sources: S&P 500 growth derived from Robert Shiller's public data; price indexes from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (not seasonally adjusted; October 2025 was not collected and is filled with the average of September and November); home prices from the FHFA, purchase-only, not seasonally adjusted.

How it works

Each category is a price index: a number that tracks what the same basket of goods or services costs over time. A change from 100 to 146 means prices rose 46%.

The page compares the latest month with the same month in the year you choose, so seasonal swings, such as summer gasoline prices, cancel out. Overall prices are the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U), all items. The difference between the two changes is given in percentage points.

Most categories come from the Consumer Price Index and measure what households pay. The contractor categories are producer prices: what contractors charge for work on nonresidential buildings. They are the closest official measure of trade prices, but they are not household repair bills.

The indexes are not seasonally adjusted. BLS collected no prices for October 2025 because of the federal government shutdown, so that month is missing and the chart line breaks there. Recent producer price values are preliminary and may be revised.

The data is stored with the page and updated by hand from the BLS. Nothing you select is sent anywhere.
