String art

One continuous thread, 5,000 straight lines, 288 nails on a circle. The nail sequence below was computed offline in Python; your browser just replays it.

parameters
nails288
lines5,000
strength0.08 per pass
skip20 neighbours
gamma1.8
target500 × 500 px
runtime≈ 6 s, numpy

algorithm

Nails sit evenly around a circle over a grayscale photo. Starting at nail 0, the program tries a straight line from the current nail to every other allowed nail and measures the average darkness of the pixels each line crosses. It keeps the darkest line, “spends” that darkness by lightening those pixels, moves to the chosen nail, and repeats. The output is just a list of nail numbers: the order to wind the thread.

changes from the original script

The original string_art.py used 100 nails and stopped at 350 lines. The result read as abstract. This run keeps the same greedy idea and changes the following:

  • Precomputed line pixels. Pixel indices for every nail pair are computed once, instead of re-rasterising each candidate line into a full-size array at every step. That is what makes thousands of lines practical.
  • Lighten, don’t erase. A chosen line lightens its pixels by a fixed strength (0.08) instead of setting them to white, so dark regions can attract many threads and tone builds up.
  • Neighbour skip. Nails within 20 positions of the current one are excluded, which avoids short chords hugging the rim.
  • Preprocessing. Square face crop, autocontrast plus gamma 1.8, and a circular mask.
  • Fixed budget. 5,000 lines, rather than the original cap and moving-average stop.
