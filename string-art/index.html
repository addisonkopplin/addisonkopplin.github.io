String Art Maker

Turn a photo into string art: thread wound in straight lines between nails around a frame. Choose a circle, square, rectangle or oval frame, and up to three thread colors from DMC embroidery floss. This tool computes the order in which to wind each thread, shows the result, and prints a nail template and the winding sequence.

Your photo is processed in your browser and never uploaded.

Photo

Drop a photo here

Drag the photo to position it inside the frame. Faces work best when they fill most of the frame. JPEG, PNG and WebP files work in every browser.

    How it works

    Nails are placed at equal spacing along the edge of the frame: a circle, square, rectangle or oval. Nail 0 is at the middle of the right side, and the numbers increase counter-clockwise.

    Each thread starts at nail 0. At each step, the program tests a straight line from the current nail to every other nail. It picks the line that crosses the most area that still needs this thread, reduces that need under the line in its working copy of the photo, and moves to the chosen nail. It repeats this for the chosen number of lines. Lines between nails that are close together, and lines between nails on the same straight side, are skipped, because they would run along the edge of the frame.

    With one thread, a dark thread covers the dark parts of the photo and a light thread on a dark board covers the light parts.

    With two or three threads, the program first maps the photo’s brightness onto the range the chosen threads and board can show. For every part of the photo, it then works out how much of each thread color is needed so that the threads, mixed over the board color, come closest to the photo’s color. It uses the screen colors of the chosen DMC floss and board for this. It then runs the line-by-line process once for each thread, in winding order. A line scores lower where it crosses areas that need the other threads more. The lines are shared out between the threads in proportion to how much of the photo each one covers. Each thread is a separate winding with its own nail order.

    Thread colors are DMC six-strand embroidery floss, named by DMC number. A picked color changes to the DMC floss closest to it in perceived color. Screen colors are approximate. The shopping list adds up the length of every line at the frame size you set, plus 10% for knots and winding around the nails, and rounds up to whole 8 m skeins.
